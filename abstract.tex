{\large\bfseries The Tell: Which Pitch-Sequencing Giveaways Survive New Games, and Why Hitters Leave Them on the Table}

\textbf{Introduction.} Advance scouts sell sequencing tells (``after a slider he doubles up''),
and clubs act on them in hitter meetings and pitcher development. No public method separates a real
tell from a pattern found by searching hundreds of pitchers, counts and previous pitches. We ask which pitchers give away the next pitch beyond count and batter hand, how much of a
scouted tell survives into new games, whether hitters punish it, and whether the 2026 automated
ball-strike (ABS) system changed it.

\textbf{Methods.} We use all 2,049,352 regular-season Statcast pitches from 2024--2026 (1,793
pitcher-seasons). For each pitcher-season we fit nested predictive models of pitch type (batter
hand, then count, then the previous pitch) with hierarchical Dirichlet smoothing, scored
leave-one-game-out. The out-of-sample gain from the previous pitch is TELL, in bits per pitch. A
concrete tell is the count, hand and previous-pitch situation that shifts one pitch type most. We
select it on April--June or on 2025 and score it only on later games. Permutation e-values,
multiplied game by game, form an in-season alarm whose chance of ever falsely firing is at most 5\%.
Cost regressions use pitcher-season and pitch-type-by-count fixed effects. ABS is evaluated on
paired pitchers against a 2024--25 placebo.

\textbf{Results.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.05em}\setlength{\topsep}{0.1em}
\item Batter hand and count carry 102 and 58 millibits about the next pitch; the previous pitch adds
8.1. A hitter guessing the likeliest pitch gains 0.6 points of accuracy on average, and 5 against
Dylan Cease.
\item Selection inflates tells. Each pitcher's largest April--June tell (26 points) keeps 10 points
in July--September, in the same direction 78\% of the time (Figure 1B). From 2025 to 2026: 28 to 13
points, 79\%. Doubling-up tells hold best (14 versus 8 points within 2025).
\item The ten largest 2025 tells with enough 2026 data all kept their direction (Table 1).
\item TELL is reliable within a season (split-half 0.73) but not across seasons ($r = 0.19$, versus
0.62 for count usage).
\item Hitters do not cash in. Per bit of predictability, pitches drew 1.5 more points of whiffs
($t = 4.7$) and 0.59 more runs saved per 100 pitches ($t = 5.3$) (Figure 1C).
\item The alarm fired for 24\% of 2025 pitchers, at a median of 290 pitches (Figure 1D).
\item ABS lowered the top of the called zone by 1.2 inches (placebo 0.2) and moved TELL by
$-0.04$ millibits (95\% CI $-1.47$ to $+1.39$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Conclusion.} Sequencing tells are real, season-specific, and less than half as large in new
games as when found. Scouts should discount them accordingly. Breaking them is not worth development time at
observed levels. Both can use an alarm that flags a detectable
pattern with a guaranteed false-alarm rate. Code: github.com/aidaneichman/the-tell.
