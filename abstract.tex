{\large\bfseries The Tell: Which Pitch-Sequencing Giveaways Survive New Games}

\textbf{Introduction.} Advance scouts sell sequencing tells (``after a slider he doubles up''), and
clubs act on them. Nothing public separates real tells from search artifacts. We ask how much the previous pitch gives away beyond count and
batter hand, how much of a scouted tell survives into new games, whether predictability costs
pitchers, and whether it changed in 2026, the first automated ball-strike (ABS) challenge season.

\textbf{Methods.} All \nPitches{} Statcast pitches, 2024--2026 (\nPS{} pitcher-seasons
with \minN+ pitches). Nested models predict pitch type from batter hand, then count, then
previous pitch, on unseen games. TELL is the previous-pitch gain per pitcher-season, over
pitches after a plate appearance's first. A cue is the count, hand and previous pitch that most
shifts one pitch type's share. Each pitcher's top cue is found in April--June (or 2025) and scored
only on later games, and net of the league. Outcome regressions fix pitcher-season,
pitch type $\times$ count $\times$ hand and velocity; a second adds the previous pitch's result. An in-season alarm multiplies game-by-game
permutation e-values within count and batter hand. 2025--26 changes are compared with 2024--25; TELL changes
use pitchers seen in both years.

\textbf{Results.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.05em}\setlength{\topsep}{0.1em}
\item In order, hand and count carry \mbHand{} and \mbCount{} millibits; the previous
pitch adds \mbTell. Guessing the likeliest pitch, a hitter gains \guessGain{} points of accuracy,
\guessTopGain{} against \guessTopName.
\item The top April--June cue shifts the next-pitch share by \hoIn{} points
when found and \hoOut{} on July--September games (\hoSign\% same direction; no tell: 0, 50\%). Net of
the league, \hoNet{} points (\hoNetSign\%; Figure~1B).
\item The \tabN{} largest 2025 cues all kept direction in 2026, shrinking \tabIn{} to
\tabOut{} points (Table~1).
\item TELL's within-season split-half reliability is \relHalf; across seasons, $r = \yoyTell$
(count usage \yoyCount).
\item Predictability is not associated with worse outcomes. From its 10th to 90th percentile,
whiffs rise \effWhiff{} points ($t = \tWhiff$) and pitchers save \effRv{} more runs per 100 pitches
($t = \tRv$); controlling the previous pitch's result, \effWhiffPo{} and \effRvPo.
\item The alarm fired for \almFire\% of 2025 pitchers (median \almMed{} pitches), \almCtrl\% of
cue-free controls, and \almSim\% of simulations with the count responding to pitches (Figure~1C).
\item In 2026 the called zone's top was \topAbs{} inches lower
(2024$\to$25: \topPlac); TELL changed \tellAbsDiff{} millibits (95\% CI \tellAbsLo{} to
\tellAbsHi; 2024$\to$25: \tellPlacDiff). The zone moved; sequencing did not.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Conclusion.} Sequencing tells are real but shrink by a third to two-thirds when held out,
and overall predictability is far less stable across seasons than within one. Clubs should validate cues
on held-out games, discount them, and track them with the alarm.
Data and code: \mbox{github.com/aidaneichman/the-tell}.
